# Start Here: Your Brain Isn't Broken. It's Trying to Help, Badly.

It's 11:40 at night. You sent a message at 6:15, and the reply came at 9:02. It says
"sure."

Not "sure!" Not "sounds great." Just "sure," with a period.

You've read it four times. You've scrolled up to check what you wrote. You've drafted two
follow-ups and deleted both. You know, somewhere in the back of your mind, that this is
probably nothing. And you're still lying there, turning it over like a stone.

If that sounds familiar, here's the first thing I want you to know: **your brain isn't
broken.** It's doing what brains do. It's trying to protect you by going over things,
checking for danger, getting ready. The trouble is that somewhere along the way, the
going-over stopped being useful and started going in circles.

That's what this book is about: telling the useful kind of thinking from the circling kind,
and getting out of the circle when you're in it.

## Thinking isn't the problem

You've probably heard advice like "stop thinking so much" or "just don't think about it."
If that worked, you wouldn't be reading this.

Thinking isn't the enemy. Going over a problem can help you plan, prepare and recover from
something hard. Researchers who study repetitive thinking describe two kinds: the
constructive kind, which moves you toward a plan or a decision, and the unconstructive kind,
which circles and keeps you stuck.[^S1]

Overthinking is the second kind. It feels like problem-solving. It isn't. It's a loop, and
loops have a way out.

## What this book is

This is a playbook, not a lecture. It's built to be used in the moment, when you're stuck.

- **Part 1 (Chapters 1 to 3)** gives you the method. You'll learn the **2-Minute Unspiral**,
  a short sequence for getting out of any loop, and **eight tools**, each matched to the
  kind of spiral it works best on. Read this part first. It takes about 20 minutes.
- **Part 2 (Chapters 4 to 10)** is the playbook itself: 27 real moments, from the
  unanswered text to the 3 a.m. to-do list, each with the tool to use and what to say to
  yourself. Don't read it cover to cover. Find your spiral and use the card.
- **Part 3 (Chapter 11)** is a simple 21-day plan that turns the tools into habits.

Every moment in Part 2 uses the same layout, and the words to say to yourself are in
**bold**, so you can find them fast, even at 2 a.m.

## What this book is not

This book is for everyday overthinking: the replays, what-ifs and stuck decisions that most
people know well. It is not therapy, and it can't diagnose anything.

Sometimes worry is more than a habit. If it's there most days, if it's affecting your
sleep, work or relationships, or if you're dealing with panic or a low mood that won't lift,
please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. Chapter 10 lists the signs. Getting
help isn't failing at this book. It's using every tool you have.

## A few notes before you start

**Expect fewer spirals, not zero.** Your mind will still wander into loops. The goal is
shorter loops and a faster way out.

**Use your own words.** The lines in bold are starting points. Change them until they sound
like you.

**The people in this book are illustrative.** Rachel, Tom, Sofia and the others are
composites, built from moments almost everyone recognizes. They are not real people.

## Your first step

Think about the last time you got stuck in your head. Was it something you said? Something
that might happen? A choice you couldn't make?

Find it in the **Find Your Spiral** list at the front of this book and mark it. Then read
Chapter 1. It's short, and you can use it on the very next loop.
