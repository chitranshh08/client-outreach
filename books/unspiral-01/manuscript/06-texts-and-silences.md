# Chapter 5: Texts, Silences and "What Did They Mean?"

Priya is 27. On Thursday she messaged a new friend from her running group: "Want to grab
coffee this weekend?" The little word under the message changed to "Seen" at 4:12 p.m.

It's now Friday night. No reply.

In 28 hours, Priya has built a whole story. The friend didn't really like her. The friend
is annoyed about something Priya said on the run last week. The friend is showing the
message to someone else right now and they're both laughing. Priya has opened the chat
eleven times to check whether the friend is typing.

Here's what actually happened, which Priya will find out on Sunday: the friend read the
message on a train, meant to answer when she got home, and forgot.

Texts and silences are a special kind of spiral, because they give your mind almost
nothing to work with. A few words, or none. A time stamp. And a mind that hates a blank
will fill it, usually with the worst story available.

The cards in this chapter all work the same way: **notice the story you're writing, list
the other stories, and take one action or none.** Then put the phone down.

---

## Moment 7: The unanswered text

**The spiral:** What-If, often mixed with a Replay of what you sent.

**What's going on:** No reply feels like information. It almost never is. People don't
answer for a hundred ordinary reasons: busy, driving, at work, meant to and forgot, waiting
until they have a proper answer.

**Try this:** Best–Worst–Likely, the text version.

- **Worst:** "They're upset with me."
- **Best:** "They're excited and want to answer properly."
- **Most likely:** "They're busy and will answer later."

Then plan for the likely case only. Plan: wait. That's it.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"No reply isn't a reply. It's just no information yet."**

**If it keeps going:** Give the chat a time. Decide when you'll check again ("after dinner")
and close the app until then. If it's been a few days and it matters, one light follow-up
is fine: **"Just bumping this up in case it got buried."**

**One small step:** Turn the phone face down, or put it in another room for twenty minutes.

---

## Moment 8: "Seen" and no reply

**The spiral:** What-If.

**What's going on:** "Seen" feels worse than plain silence, because now you know they read
it. But "seen" only means the message was opened. It doesn't mean they've decided anything
about you. Plenty of people read a message so it stops nagging them, then answer later.

**Try this:** Name It, then list three other stories.

1. Name it: **"What-If. I feel rejected."**
2. Write down three ordinary reasons someone might read and not reply. (Read it in a
   meeting. Wanted to check their calendar first. Opened it by accident while scrolling.)
3. Notice that you can't know which one is true, and neither can your loop.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"I'm writing a story with no facts in it."**

**If it keeps going:** Watch the checking. Opening the chat again and again to see if
they're typing keeps the loop fed. If you need a rule, make it this: **one check per
hour, and none after bedtime.**

**One small step:** Mute the chat until tomorrow morning. You'll still see the reply when
it comes.

---

## Moment 9: The short reply: "ok."

**The spiral:** What-If, with a side of Replay.

**What's going on:** Short messages carry very little tone, so your mind adds its own.
Tired, anxious minds tend to add a cold one. "ok." might mean "fine, whatever." It might
also mean "ok," typed in an elevator with one thumb.

**Try this:** Best–Worst–Likely, then one honest check.

- **Worst:** "They're annoyed."
- **Best:** "It's fine. They agree."
- **Most likely:** "They're busy, and 'ok' means ok."

If the message matters and you're still unsure after that, ask once, lightly:
**"Just checking: are we good for Thursday?"** A real question gets you a real answer, which
is better than a hundred guesses.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"Short isn't the same as upset."**

**If it keeps going:** Look at what you're asking for. If you've already checked once and
they said it's fine, asking again won't bring lasting relief. The relief lasts a few
minutes, then the question comes back. Take their answer as the answer.

**One small step:** Reply to their message (or not), then do the next thing on your list.

---

## Moment 10: Waiting to hear back

*The interview, the application, the test results, the offer*

**The spiral:** What-If.

**What's going on:** Waiting is a long stretch of not knowing, and your mind tries to end
it early by guessing. Every hour without news gets read as bad news.

**Try this:** The Worry Window, with the waiting inside it.

1. Write down the date you're likely to hear. If you don't know it, it's fine to ask once:
   **"Could you tell me roughly when you expect to decide?"**
2. Do Best–Worst–Likely once, on paper. Make one plan for the worst case: "If it's a no,
   I'll apply to the next two on my list."
3. Every time the waiting spiral starts, park it: **"That's for the window."** In your
   Worry Window, check whether anything has changed. Usually it hasn't.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"Waiting isn't a job. I don't have to do it all day."**

**If it keeps going:** Fill the gaps. Waiting spirals live in empty time, so plan a few
absorbing things for the days you're waiting: a long walk, a film, a project with steps.

**One small step:** Write the "if it's a no" plan now, in one line. Then close the email
app.

---

## Moment 11: Silence after you posted something

**The spiral:** What-If, often turning into a Replay of the post itself.

**What's going on:** You shared something, and the likes are slow, or there's no reply in
the group chat. Your mind treats it like a vote. But most posts get little attention for
reasons that have nothing to do with you: timing, an algorithm, people busy with their own
day.

**Try this:** Solve or Park.

Ask: *Is there one thing I can do about this in the next 24 hours?* Almost always the
answer is no. You can't make people react. So park it, and close the app.

The one exception: if you posted something you now regret, the action is simple. Edit it,
or delete it. Then it's done.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"Quiet isn't a verdict."**

**If it keeps going:** Set a rule before you post next time: post, then close the app for
an hour. Checking every few minutes turns a post into a test.

**One small step:** Close the app and leave it closed until tomorrow.

---

## One Small Step

**Make your own "check later" rule.** Choose one: no checking messages after a set time at
night, or a set time to look at the chat ("after dinner"), or muting chats that tend to
start a spiral. Write it down. A rule decided in advance beats a decision made in the
middle of a loop.
