# Chapter 9: The People Spirals

Ben is 36. At a friend's birthday on Saturday, he met someone new, a friend of a friend,
and they talked for twenty minutes. On the way home, Ben started wondering what she
thought of him. Did he seem interesting? Did he talk too much about work? Was the laugh at
the end a real laugh or a polite one?

On Sunday morning, he opened Instagram and saw photos from the same party. Everyone looked
happy and relaxed. He looked, he decided, tired and awkward. Then he scrolled on, past a
colleague's new house and an old school friend's promotion, and by the time he put the
phone down, he felt worse about his weekend, his job and his face.

People spirals mix all three types. There's a Replay (what did I say?), a What-If (what do
they think?) and a constant comparison that never ends in a decision. What they share is
this: **they're all about things inside other people's heads,** which you can't see and
can't control.

---

## Moment 26: "What do they think of me?"

**The spiral:** Replay and What-If together.

**What's going on:** Your mind is trying to read other people's opinions from tiny clues: a
pause, a look, a reply that took too long. It feels like careful attention. Really, it's
guessing, and an anxious guess tends to be a harsh one.

Here's something that helps. Remember the T-shirt study from Chapter 4: people guessed that
about half the room had noticed their embarrassing shirt, and fewer than a quarter had.[^S10]
Researchers call this the **spotlight effect**. We tend to feel more watched than we are.

**Try this:** Name It, then the Thought Label.

1. Name it: **"People spiral. I feel self-conscious."**
2. Catch the exact thought, and put the label in front of it: **"I'm having the thought
   that she found me boring."**
3. Ask: *Do I actually know this?* If the honest answer is no, it's a guess, and guesses
   can be parked.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"Most people are thinking about themselves, not me."**

**If it keeps going:** Turn your attention outward. Next time you're in a conversation and
the spiral starts, ask the other person a question and really listen to the answer. It's
hard to watch yourself and listen to someone else at the same time.

**One small step:** If there's a real action (you'd like to see that person again), take
it: **"Great to meet you on Saturday. Coffee sometime?"** If there isn't, park it and do
something with your hands.

---

## Moment 27: Comparing yourself online

**The spiral:** A Replay of your own life, measured against everyone else's best moments.

**What's going on:** Social media shows you the highlights of hundreds of lives, edited and
chosen, next to the full, unedited version of yours. The comparison is never fair, and it
never ends in an action. You can't win it, so the loop just keeps running.

The amount of time matters. In one study, university students who cut their social media
use to about 30 minutes a day for three weeks reported less loneliness and depression than
students who carried on as usual.[^S14]

**Try this:** Change the Channel, then set a limit.

1. Notice it: **"I'm comparing again."** Then close the app. Don't finish the scroll.
2. Do something with a beginning and an end for ten to twenty minutes: a walk, a chore,
   a call, a chapter of a book.
3. Later, when you're not in the loop, set a limit you can keep: a daily time limit in your
   phone's settings, no apps in bed, or muting the accounts that most often start the
   spiral.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"I'm comparing my whole life to their best photo."**

**If it keeps going:** Use Solve or Park on the comparison itself. Is there something in it
you actually want, like a job change or more time with friends? Then there's a step you can
take in the next 24 hours. If not, it's just noise, so mute it.

**One small step:** Move your most-scrolled app off your home screen, into a folder.

---

## One Small Step

**Pick one people spiral and give it a real-world action.** Message the person you keep
wondering about. Mute the account that always sets you off. Ask one person a question
tomorrow and listen to the whole answer. People spirals shrink when you spend less time
inside your own head and more time with actual people.
