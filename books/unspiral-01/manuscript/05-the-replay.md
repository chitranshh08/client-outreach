# Part 2: The Playbook: 27 Moments

*Don't read this part cover to cover. Find your spiral, read the bold line, and use the tool.*

## How to use a moment card

Every moment in Part 2 has the same six parts, so you always know where to look.

- **The spiral:** which of the three it is: Replay, What-If or Can't-Decide.
- **What's going on:** one or two lines on why this moment hooks your mind.
- **Try this:** the tool to use, in about two minutes.
- **Say this to yourself:** one line, in bold. Change the words until they sound like you.
- **If it keeps going:** the backup, for the loop that comes back.
- **One small step:** something to do right now, with your hands, not your head.

**The 2-Minute Unspiral at a glance:** Notice ("I'm spiraling"). Name it (which spiral, and
the feeling). Solve or park ("Is there one thing I can do about this in the next 24
hours?"). One small step.

If you only have ten seconds, read the **Say this to yourself** line and take the step.

# Chapter 4: The Replay: Conversations, Mistakes and Awkward Moments

Sofia is 29. On Saturday she had dinner with three old friends, and somewhere between the
main course and dessert, she told a story about her job that went on too long. She noticed
one friend glance at her phone. She wrapped it up with a joke that didn't land.

It's now Monday. Sofia has replayed that dinner, by her own rough count, about forty times.
In the shower. On the bus. In the middle of a work call, when she should have been
listening. Each time she edits the story a little shorter, and each time she arrives at the
same place: *They must think I'm so self-absorbed.*

Nobody has said anything. Nothing has happened since Saturday. The only thing that keeps
happening is the replay.

The Replay is the most common spiral there is, and it's the one that feels most like
learning. You go over it so you won't do it again. But after the first one or two passes,
there's nothing left to learn. You're just watching the same clip, and it hurts a little
more each time.

Remember what the Replay wants: **one lesson, then permission to let go.** Every card in
this chapter gives it exactly that.

---

## Moment 1: The conversation you keep replaying

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** Your mind is searching the conversation for a mistake, so it can make
sure you never repeat it. The search has no end point, so it keeps running. Each replay
also changes the memory a little, usually toward the worst version.

**Try this:** Name It, then pull out one lesson.

1. Name the spiral and the feeling: **"Replay. I feel awkward."**
2. Ask: *Is there one lesson here?* Write it in one line. "Next time, I'll ask a question
   instead of telling a long story."
3. If there's no lesson, write that down too: "No lesson. It was a normal conversation."

**Say this to yourself:**
**"I've got the lesson. I don't need the rerun."**

**If it keeps going:** Use the Thought Label on the line that hurts most: **"I'm having the
thought that they think I'm boring."** Then change the channel for ten minutes: a walk, a
podcast, a task with steps.

**One small step:** Put your one-line lesson in your phone notes. Then close the app.

---

## Moment 2: "Why did I say that?"

*The awkward comment*

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** One sentence came out wrong, and now it plays on a loop. "Why" feels
like the right question, but it has no answer you can use. Asking it again just plays the
clip again.

It may also help to know that the moment is far bigger in your memory than in anyone
else's. In a well-known study, students who wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room
guessed that about half the people there had noticed it. Fewer than a quarter had.[^S10]
Most people are busy thinking about what *they* said.

**Try this:** Solve or Park, starting with a "what."

- Instead of **"Why did I say that?"** ask **"What, if anything, do I want to do about it?"**
- There are only three answers: say something, learn something, or let it go.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"It's louder in my head than it was in the room."**

**If it keeps going:** Try the word-repetition trick from Chapter 3. Say the word that
stings ("awkward, awkward, awkward…") out loud, quickly, for about 30 seconds, until it
turns into a sound. Somewhere private works best.

**One small step:** Decide which of the three answers it is. If it's "let it go," say
**"Done"** out loud and start the next thing on your list.

## Moment 3: The message you already sent

*The email, text or comment you can't take back*

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** You hit send, and then you read it again. And again. Now every word
looks wrong: too blunt, too long, too many exclamation marks. Rereading feels like checking.
It's actually the loop.

**Try this:** Solve or Park, with a rule: **one reread, then decide.**

1. Read the message once more, slowly, as if someone else had sent it to you.
2. Ask: *Is there a real problem?* A wrong fact, a wrong name, a missing attachment? If yes,
   send a short fix: "Quick correction: the meeting is Thursday, not Tuesday."
3. If there's no real problem, the message stays as it is. Park it.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"It's sent. It's fine. It's not mine to reread."**

**If it keeps going:** Move the message out of sight. Archive the email or close the chat.
You can't reread what you can't see. If the worry is about how it will land, that's a
What-If: give it one line in tomorrow's Worry Window.

**One small step:** Close the thread now, and open the next task.

## Moment 4: A mistake at work

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** The mistake might be small (a typo in a report) or real (a missed
deadline). Either way, your mind treats it like a threat and replays it to make sure you
"learn." But replaying it doesn't fix anything. Only actions do.

**Try this:** Solve or Park, the work version.

1. **Fix what can be fixed.** Is there one thing you can do in the next 24 hours? Correct
   the file. Tell the person affected. Say what you'll do differently.
2. **Say it once, plainly.** **"I missed that. Here's what I've done to fix it."** No long
   apology, no explaining why.
3. **Write the lesson.** One line. "Check the date column before sending."
4. **Then park the rest.** Your manager's opinion of you isn't something you can do
   anything about tonight.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"I fixed what I could. The rest is a replay."**

**If it keeps going:** Watch for the jump from "I made a mistake" to "I'm bad at my job."
That's a harsh thought, so use the Thought Label: **"I'm having the thought that I'm bad at
my job."** One mistake is one mistake.

**One small step:** Send the fix, or put it in your calendar for the morning. Then do one
unrelated task, start to finish.

## Moment 5: The argument that ended badly

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** The argument is over, but in your head it's still going. You think of
better comebacks. You replay what they said in a sharper tone each time. This replay is
often mixed with a What-If: *What if this is the end of us?*

**Try this:** Name It, then Solve or Park.

1. Name it: **"Replay. I'm hurt and I'm angry."** Both can be true.
2. Ask the solve-or-park question. Often the honest answer is: **yes, one thing.** Not
   winning the argument. Repairing it. A short message is enough:
   **"I don't like how that ended. Can we talk tomorrow?"**
3. If it's too soon, or the other person needs space, park it with a time: "I'll message
   tomorrow at lunch."

**Say this to yourself:**
**"I don't need the perfect comeback. I need one next step."**

**If it keeps going:** Stop writing new arguments in your head. If you need to get it out,
do a two-minute brain dump of what you want them to understand, as a list. Then close the
notebook. A list helps you prepare. A ten-page draft usually feeds the loop.

**One small step:** Send the repair message, or set a reminder for the time you chose.

## Moment 6: Replaying the party on the way home

*Any social event: a wedding, drinks after work, a dinner*

**The spiral:** Replay.

**What's going on:** You're tired, the social energy is gone, and your mind starts the
review: every joke, every pause, every time you talked too much or too little. Tiredness
makes the loop louder, and the review is almost always harsher than the night was.

**Try this:** Name It, then a fair review.

1. Name it: **"Replay. I'm tired and self-conscious."**
2. Make the review fair. For each moment you're replaying, find one that went fine: a laugh
   you shared, a good question you asked, someone who seemed glad you came. This isn't
   pretending. It's correcting a review that only looks for mistakes.
3. One lesson, or none. Most nights out don't have one.

**Say this to yourself:**
**"Tired brains write bad reviews. I'll read it again in the morning."**

**If it keeps going:** Park it: one line for tomorrow's Worry Window. Most replays that start
at 11 p.m. look much smaller by 11 a.m. If it's still there in the morning, run the card for
Moment 1.

**One small step:** Put on music or a podcast for the rest of the trip home. Give your mind
something else to hold.

## One Small Step

**Pick your most common Replay from this chapter.** Write its bold line on a sticky note or
your phone's lock screen. The next time that replay starts, you won't have to think of what
to say. It's already there.
