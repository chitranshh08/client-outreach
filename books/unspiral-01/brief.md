## Reader avatar
- **Who:** An adult (mostly 25–45, often a woman, often busy with work or young kids) who is
  capable and conscientious, and whose mind won't let things go.
- **Situation now:** She replays a conversation for hours. Rereads a text to decode its tone.
  Lies awake running what-ifs. Can't pick a sofa, a job or a reply without researching it to
  death. She knows it's "just overthinking," and knowing that doesn't make it stop.
- **Already tried:** Telling herself to stop. Meditation apps she abandoned. Journaling that
  turned into more rumination. A popular book that told her thinking is the problem but gave
  her no tools. Scrolling to drown it out.
- **What she wants (her words):** "I just want my brain to be quiet." "I know I'm
  overthinking. How do I actually stop?" "Make a decision and move on." "Stop replaying
  every stupid thing I said."

## The promise (one sentence)
> When your mind starts spinning, you'll know which spiral you're in and exactly which short
> tool to use, so you can stop the replay, settle the what-if or make the call, and get back
> to your day.

Not promised: "never overthink again", "in 60 seconds", "cure anxiety". Those raise clicks
and lower reviews, and the last one breaks CLAUDE.md rule 4.

## Competitor gaps → how we win
_From the niche report's review mining (2026-10-05); add more quotes if the owner sends
review screenshots._

| Complaint in competitors' reviews | Our fix |
|---|---|
| "Nothing new": the same recycled techniques (Trenton) | Organized by **moment**, not by technique: find your spiral (the unanswered text, the 2 a.m. replay) and get the tool for it |
| "Overly mechanical": 23 techniques, no guide to which one when | **8 tools only**, each matched to one of 3 spiral types (Replay, What-If, Can't-Decide), with a one-page chooser |
| "Pinterest quotes cover to cover" (Chidiac) | No quote filler; every page gives a step, a script or a worked example |
| Contradictory advice ("don't overthink by overthinking everything") | One simple model used the whole way through: notice, name, solve-or-park, act |
| Blames others ("everyone's a narcissist") | No blame, no labels for other people; the book is about your loop, not their character |
| Overreach: implies people with anxiety can therapize themselves (Trenton) | Clear limits and a "when to talk to a professional" page, with crisis resources |
| "Don't think" thesis with no tools (critics of Nguyen) | Thinking isn't the enemy; **unproductive** thinking is. The book teaches the difference and what to do instead |

## Positioning
*Don't Believe Everything You Think* (Nguyen) and *Stop Overthinking* (Trenton) sell the idea
and the menu. **Unspiral is the in-the-moment guide**: open it at the moment you're stuck,
find your spiral, use the tool. It sits next to the big books in "customers also bought",
not against them.

## Format & business decisions
- **Target word count:** ~20,000–24,000 (tight, like Calm Words Book 2). ≈ 95–115 pages at 6×9.
- **Trim size:** 6×9, white paper, B&W; same build pipeline (`tools/book_build/`).
- **KDP Select:** **Yes.** 7/10 page-1 books are in KU, and this reader borrows.
- **Price:** ebook $2.99 launch → **$4.99** (matches the self-published leader's anchor, above
  the losing $0.99 clones, below the $9.99 publisher titles) · paperback **$14.99**.
- **Quick win (first 10%):** the **2-Minute Unspiral** (Notice → Name → Solve or Park → One
  Small Step) in Chapter 1, usable on the very next spiral.
- **Extras:** "Find Your Spiral" index, a one-page Tool Chooser, the Worry Window card,
  a Decision Rule card, a 2 a.m. card, a 21-day plan, a cheat sheet of every moment's tool.

## Compliance (CLAUDE.md rule 4)
General education only: no diagnosis, no treatment claims, no "anxiety cure". Disclaimer in
the front matter; a clear "when to talk to a professional" page (persistent worry that
disrupts daily life, panic, low mood, intrusive thoughts, sleep problems lasting weeks) with
crisis line info (988 in the US). Every technique gets a real source or is presented as a
practical tool, not a research finding.
