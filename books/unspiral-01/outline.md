# Outline: Unspiral (working title)

Target: ~20,000–24,000 words (≈ 95–115 pages, 6×9) · 3 parts · 11 chapters · 27 moments
Quick win: the 2-Minute Unspiral opens Chapter 1, at ~word 1,100, inside the first 10%.

**Rules for this book (from the brief and the niche report)**
- **One model, used everywhere:** notice → name the spiral → solve or park → one small step.
- **Three spiral types**, named the way readers talk: **Replay** (the past), **What-If** (the
  future), **Can't-Decide** (a choice). Every moment card says which one it is.
- **Eight tools only**, each tied to a spiral type. No technique buffet.
- **No quotes as filler, no blame, no diagnosis.** Every fact gets a verified source in
  `sources.md`, or is cut.
- **Card format for Part 2 (always this order):** The spiral (which type) · What's going on ·
  **Try this** (the tool, in 2 minutes) · **Say this to yourself** (one bold line) · If it
  keeps going · One small step

## PART 1: HOW TO UNSPIRAL

### Chapter 1: The 2-Minute Unspiral (~1,800) ← quick win
- Vignette: the replay after a work meeting
- **The 2-Minute Unspiral:** Notice ("I'm spiraling") · Name it (Replay / What-If /
  Can't-Decide) · **Solve or Park?** · One small step
- **Solve or Park:** is there one action I can take in the next 24 hours? If yes, do or schedule
  it. If no, park it in tomorrow's Worry Window
- **One Small Step:** the end-of-chapter action
- Extra: pocket card

### Chapter 2: Why Your Mind Loops (~1,800)
- Productive vs unproductive repetitive thinking: problem-solving moves forward, rumination
  circles (S: Watkins 2008)
- Why the loop feels useful (it *feels* like preparing) and why it rarely pays off
- What makes it worse: tiredness, late night, scrolling, vague "why" questions
- What doesn't work: "just stop thinking" (thought suppression tends to backfire; S: Wegner
  "white bear" research)
- **One Small Step:** catch and name 3 spirals today (no fixing)

### Chapter 3: Your Eight Tools (and Which One to Use When) (~2,600)
| Tool | Best for | One line |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Worry Window** | What-If | Park it for a fixed 15-minute slot tomorrow (S: stimulus-control worry research) |
| 2. **Solve or Park** | All | One action in 24 h? Do it. If not, park it |
| 3. **Name It** | All | "This is the Replay again." Labeling turns down the volume (S: affect labeling) |
| 4. **The Thought Label** | Replay | "I'm having the thought that…" (S: cognitive defusion research) |
| 5. **Brain Dump** | All, at night | Two minutes on paper, then close the notebook (S: expressive writing / pre-sleep worry list) |
| 6. **Best–Worst–Likely** | What-If | Write all three; plan only for "likely" |
| 7. **Good-Enough Rule** | Can't-Decide | Set your must-haves, pick the first option that meets them (S: satisficing research) |
| 8. **Change the Channel** | Replay | A short absorbing task or a walk; distraction beats rumination in the moment (S: Nolen-Hoeksema) |
- **The Tool Chooser** (one page): spiral type → first tool → backup tool
- **One Small Step:** set tomorrow's Worry Window time
- Extra: Tool Chooser card · Worry Window card

  thoughts, sleep problems lasting weeks, or it's affecting work or relationships. Crisis
  lines (988 in the US). General info only (S: NIMH / NHS pages on generalized worry)
