# Chapter 12: Your 14-Day Listening Challenge

You now have everything in this book: the One Clear Ask, the Follow-Through Ladder, 30
scripts for everyday moments, and the routines that cut down how often you need to ask at
all.

That's a lot to take in. The easiest way to lose it is to try everything at once for two
days and then slide back into the old loop. So this chapter turns the book into a simple
two-week challenge: **one small step a day, building to the full method.**

## How the challenge works

- **Each day has one small action**, usually five minutes or less.
- **Missed a day? Just carry on.** Don't restart. The challenge works even when you do it
  imperfectly.
- **Count on Day 1 and Day 14.** One hour of tallying at the start and the end, so you can
  see what changed.
- **Check in every few days.** A couple of quick questions help you notice progress, even
  when it's slow.

---

## Days 1 to 3: Make the first ask count

*Focus: the One Clear Ask (Chapter 1)*

- **Day 1:** Count for one busy hour. A mark for every ask, a circle for every repeat. Write
  the totals in the tracker below. No changes yet. Just count.
- **Day 2:** Pick one request you make every day. Use all five steps for it, every time:
  get close, get their eyes, say it short, wait, follow through.
- **Day 3:** Listen for questions and "okay?" in your asks. Swap each one for a short, kind
  statement: **"Shoes on, please."**

**Check-in:** Which of the five steps is hardest for me? (Often it's walking over, or
waiting.)

---

## Days 4 to 7: Never stuck after "no"

*Focus: the Follow-Through Ladder (Chapter 3)*

- **Day 4:** Fill in your Consequence Picker for your three hardest moments.
- **Day 5:** Practice Rung 1 every time it's needed: one reminder, with a choice.
- **Day 6:** Use helping hands or "When… then…" instead of a third ask.
- **Day 7:** Catch your child listening five times today, and say exactly what they did.
  **"You came the first time I called. Thank you."**

**Check-in:** Did I reach for a threat or a yell less often this week? Which rung do I use
most?

---

## Days 8 to 11: Your moments

*Focus: the script cards (Chapters 4 to 9)*

- **Day 8:** Look at your Day 1 tally. Pick the three moments that cost you the most repeats,
  and find them in the Find Your Moment list.
- **Day 9:** Read the **Say this** lines for all three out loud, twice. Then use one.
- **Day 10:** Use the other two.
- **Day 11:** For a moment that isn't in the book, build your own: One Clear Ask, then the
  ladder.

**Check-in:** Is one of my three moments easier than it was a week ago?

---

## Days 12 to 14: Fewer asks

*Focus: rules and routines (Chapter 11)*

- **Day 12:** Write your family rules together and put the poster up.
- **Day 13:** Make a picture routine chart or a "When… then…" chart for your hardest time
  of day. Use **"What's next on the chart?"** instead of an instruction.
- **Day 14:** Count again, for the same busy hour as Day 1. Write the totals in the
  tracker.

---

## Your Day 14 check-in

Compare your Day 1 and Day 14 numbers, then answer honestly. There are no wrong answers.

1. Am I giving **fewer instructions** than two weeks ago?
2. Am I **repeating myself less**? (Look at the circles.)
3. Is my child **starting sooner** after I ask, at least some of the time?
4. Do **I** yell less, even on hard days?
5. Which **step or rung** made the biggest difference?
6. Which moment is **still hard**? (Go back to its card, or check Chapter 10.)

If you see even a small change, that's real progress. A child who used to need five asks
and now needs two has learned something important: *when this grown-up asks, it happens.*
That's the whole goal. Keep the tools that work, drop the ones that don't, and come back to
this book whenever a new stage brings new battles.

And if, after two weeks of steady effort, listening is getting harder instead of easier, or
something worries you, look at the signs in Chapter 10 and talk to your pediatrician.
Asking for help is part of the plan, not a failure of it.
