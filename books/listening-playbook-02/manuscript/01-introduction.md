# Start Here: You're Not Being Ignored. Your Words Are Getting Lost.

"Shoes on, please."

Nothing.

"Shoes on. We're leaving."

Nothing. Your five-year-old is lying on the rug, making two plastic dinosaurs fight.

"Did you hear me? SHOES."

By the fifth time, you're standing over him and your voice has turned into the voice you
promised yourself you'd stop using. And *now* he moves. He looks a little scared, he
puts his shoes on, and you walk to the car feeling like the worst version of yourself.

If that sounds like your house, here's the first thing I want you to know: **you're not a
bad parent.** You're stuck in a loop that almost every
family falls into. In one national survey, 42% of parents of young children said they
wish they didn't yell or raise their voice as quickly as they do.[^S6] You are in very
large company.

And the loop can be broken. Not with more patience, more charts or a stricter voice. With
a different way of asking, and a calm plan for what happens next.

## Why your words get lost

Most of the time, children don't tune us out to be rude. Our words get lost on the way.

We ask from across the room while they're deep in play. We ask a question ("Can you put
your shoes on?") when we mean an instruction. We say five things at once. And then,
after the first ask, nothing happens. No one walks over. No one helps. So the first ask
starts to mean "not yet," and the fifth one, the loud one, starts to mean "now."

Chapter 2 explains this in more detail, and it's one of the most freeing ideas in the
book: **yelling isn't what makes children listen.** It just happens to arrive at the
same moment you finally get up and follow through. Keep the follow-through, drop the
yell, and you get the listening without the guilt.

## What this book is

This is a playbook, not a textbook. It's built to be used, not just read.

- **Part 1 (Chapters 1 to 3)** gives you the method. You'll learn the **One Clear Ask**,
  five small steps that make your first ask the one that counts, and the
  **Follow-Through Ladder**, your calm plan for when your child still doesn't do it. Read
  this part first. It takes about 20 minutes, and you can use it at the next "shoes on."
- **Part 2 (Chapters 4 to 10)** is the playbook itself: 30 everyday moments, from "In a
  minute!" to "You can't make me!", each with the exact words to say. Don't read it cover
  to cover. Find your moment and use it.
- **Part 3 (Chapters 11 and 12)** helps you need fewer reminders in the first place, with
  family rules, routines and a 14-day challenge that puts it all together.

Every moment in Part 2 follows the same layout. The words to say are always in **bold**,
and every moment ends with what to do **if they still don't**, so you're never left
standing there wondering what comes next.

## Which book do you need?

This is Book 2 in the Calm Words Parenting series. Book 1, *The Meltdown Playbook*, is for
the moments when your child is upset: screaming, crying, kicking, overwhelmed. This book
is for the moments when your child is fine, but won't do what you asked.

**Upset child? Feelings first.** That's *The Meltdown Playbook*.
**Calm child who won't do it? Ask once, follow through.** That's this book.

You don't need Book 1 to use this one. Where the two meet, I'll point you to the right
page.

## What this book is not

This book is for everyday listening and cooperation in children aged about 2 to 7. It is
not therapy, and it can't diagnose anything. Some children find listening hard for
reasons no script can fix, and that is nobody's fault. Chapter 10 explains the signs that
it's time to talk to your pediatrician.

It also isn't a promise that your child will listen the first time, every time. No child
does, and no adult does either. The goal is **fewer repeats**, a calmer voice, and a
child who learns that when you ask, you mean it.

## A few notes before you start

**The scripts are starting points.** Change the words so they sound like you. A script
you actually say beats a perfect one you forget.

**Expect some testing.** When you change how you ask, many children check whether the
new way is real. Use the same words and the same follow-through for a week before you
judge whether it's working.

**The families in this book are illustrative.** Theo, Isla, Eli and the other children
you'll meet are composites, built from moments almost every family knows. They are not
real people.

**You don't have to be perfect.** You'll still raise your voice sometimes. Every parent
does. The method works even if you start it on your third ask instead of your first.

## Your first step

Think about the last few days at home. Which request did you have to repeat the most?
Shoes? Cleanup? Coming to the table? Stopping something?

Find it in the **Find Your Moment** list at the front of this book and mark it. That's the
moment we'll work on first.

Then read Chapter 1. It's short, and you can try it before the day is over.
