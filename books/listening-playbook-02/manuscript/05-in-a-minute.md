# Part 2: The Playbook: 30 Moments, 30 Scripts

*Don't read this part cover to cover. Find your moment, read the bold line, and go.*

## How to use a script card

Every moment in Part 2 has the same six parts, so you always know where to look.

- **What's going on:** one or two lines on why this moment is hard for your child.
- **Say this:** the ask. Use the One Clear Ask: get close, get their eyes, then say it.
- **If they still don't:** your next steps on the Follow-Through Ladder.
- **Instead of / Try:** a common phrase that backfires, and a swap.
- **Age tweaks:** small changes for ages 2–3, 4–5 and 6–7.
- **Next time:** one idea to make the moment easier before it starts.

The bold lines are the words to say. Everything in brackets is what to *do*. And after
every ask, wait. Count to five in your head before anything else.

If you only have ten seconds, read the **Say this** line and go.

# Chapter 4: "In a Minute!": When They Tune You Out

Hazel is four. She's at the kitchen table with a pile of stickers, and her mom has asked
her to get her coat three times. Each time, Hazel says "Okay!" and keeps sticking.

It's not that Hazel is being rude. She really does mean "okay." She just means it in the
way four-year-olds do: *okay, at some point, once this sticker is on.*

The moments in this chapter are the quiet ones. No screaming, no stomping. Just a child
who doesn't seem to hear you, or hears you and doesn't move. They're also the moments
where the repeat loop starts, which makes them the best place to break it.

---

## Moment 1: "Did you hear me?"

*Ignoring you in the middle of play*

**What's going on:** Your child's whole attention is on the game. Your voice may not even
register as something they need to answer, especially from across the room. Switching
attention away from play is a skill they're still building.

**Say this:**
[Walk over. Crouch down. Say their name and wait for the look.]
**"That's a big sticker picture. Coat on, please."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"Coat on. Do you want to put it on by yourself or with my help?"**
- *Rung 2:* **"Let's do it together."** [Hold the coat open and guide one arm in.]
- *Rung 4:* **"Coat's on. Thank you. Let's go."**

**Instead of:** "Did you hear me? I said COAT!" (from the hallway)
**Try:** [Walk over.] **"Hazel. Look at me. Coat on, please."**

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** Use one or two words and a gesture. Point at the coat: **"Coat on."**
- **4–5:** Let them finish one small thing first: **"One more sticker, then coat."**
- **6–7:** Ask them to repeat it back: **"What's next?"** "Coat." **"Yep."**

**Next time:** Give a heads-up before the ask. **"Two more stickers, then it's coat time."**
A concrete number is easier to follow than "in a few minutes."

---

## Moment 2: "Come here!"

*Calling them to you, or time to come inside*

**What's going on:** "Come here" means stopping, leaving and walking away, all at once. When
it means coming in from outside, it also means the fun is over. Calling from the back
door makes it easy to pretend they didn't hear.

**Say this:**
[Walk out to them. Get close.]
**"Time to come inside, please. Snack is ready."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"Inside now. Do you want to race me to the door or walk like a dinosaur?"**
- *Rung 2:* **"When you're inside, then we'll have the snack."** Or take their hand and walk in
  together.
- *Rung 3:* **"Coming in was hard today, so after snack we'll play inside."**

**Instead of:** "If you don't come right now, I'm leaving without you!"
**Try:** **"I'm coming to get you. Let's go in together."**

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** Make it a game: crouch down, open your arms and say **"Run to me!"**
- **4–5:** Give them a job for the walk in: **"Can you carry the shovel?"**
- **6–7:** Agree on the signal ahead of time: **"When I ring the bell, it's time to come in."**

**Next time:** Practice "come here" as a game when nothing is at stake. Call, and when
they come, give a high five and send them back to play. Children learn that coming to you
doesn't always mean the fun ends.

## Moment 3: "In a minute!"

*The stall*

**What's going on:** Your child really does want to finish what they're doing. That's
reasonable. The trouble is that "a minute" has no end, so it turns into ten.

**Say this:**
**"You want to finish. One more page, then teeth."**
[Wait while they finish the page.]
**"That was the last page. Teeth now, please."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"Teeth now. Do you want to hop to the bathroom or tiptoe?"**
- *Rung 2:* **"I'll walk with you."** [Take their hand.]
- *Rung 4:* No lecture about the stalling. **"Teeth are done. Story time."**

**Instead of:** "Fine, five more minutes." (And then forgetting, and saying it again.)
**Try:** **"Let's set the timer together. When it beeps, it's teeth time."**

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** "One more" works better than any minutes. **"One more push of the car, then teeth."**
- **4–5:** A visual timer they can watch helps them see the time running out.
- **6–7:** Let them suggest the finish line: **"Do you want to finish the page or the
  chapter?"** Then hold them to it.

**Next time:** Give the warning *before* they have to ask for more time. Kids ask for "a
minute" less when they already know where the finish line is.

## Moment 4: "Stop that!"

*Jumping on the couch, banging, poking*

**What's going on:** Young children often have big energy and few ideas for where to put
it. "Stop!" tells them what not to do, but not what to do instead.

**Say this:**
[Walk over.]
**"Feet on the floor, please. Jumping is for outside."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"Feet on the floor. You can jump on the cushions on the floor, or outside."**
- *Rung 2:* **"I'll help you down."** [Lift them down calmly.]
- *Rung 3:* **"The couch is closed for now. You can sit on it again after lunch."**

**Instead of:** "Stop it! Stop! I said STOP!"
**Try:** Say what to do. **"Gentle hands on the dog." "Drums are for the pots, not the
window." "Walking feet inside."**

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** Move them, then show them. **"Jump here!"** and jump on the cushion with them.
- **4–5:** Offer a choice of where the energy can go: **"Jump on the cushions or dance with
  me?"**
- **6–7:** Explain once: **"The couch springs break. The floor cushions don't."**

**Next time:** Plan an energy outlet for the times it usually happens, like a "jump spot"
with cushions on the floor, or a dance song before dinner.

## Moment 5: "But Dad lets me!"

*Listens to one parent (or the sitter) but not the other*

**What's going on:** Children notice which adult follows through and which one repeats.
They aren't choosing a favorite. They're reading the pattern. If one adult asks once and
then helps, and the other asks five times, the child learns to wait for the fifth time
with that adult.

**Say this:**
[To your child, using the One Clear Ask.]
**"Shoes on, please. I'll wait."**

[To the other adult, later, when you're both calm.]
**"Can we use the same words for shoes and bedtime this week? Ask once, then help."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"Shoes on. Red ones or blue ones?"**
- *Rung 2:* **"I'll help."** If the other parent is there, they back you up without taking
  over: **"Mom asked. I'll wait with you."**

**Instead of:** "Wait till your father gets home!"
**Try:** **"I asked you. I'll help you do it."** (You don't need anyone else to follow through.)

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** Use the exact same few words as the other adult. "Shoes on" from both of you.
- **4–5:** If they say "Dad lets me!", answer once: **"With me, the rule is…"**
- **6–7:** Agree on a family rule together and say it the same way: **"In our family, we…"**

**Next time:** Pick three phrases every adult in the house will use, plus one consequence
you'll all follow through on. Chapter 10 has more on getting the grown-ups on the same
page.

## Moment 6: "Can I? Can I? Can I?"

*The broken record*

**What's going on:** If asking ten times has ever worked, asking ten times is a smart plan.
Your child is checking whether "no" is really no.

**Say this:**
[Answer once, clearly.]
**"The answer is no for today. You can ask me again tomorrow."**

**If they still don't:**
- *Rung 1:* **"I heard you. My answer is the same."**
- *Rung 2:* **"Asked and answered."** Then turn your attention to something else. The CDC
  suggests redirecting your attention for minor behaviors like whining or interrupting,
  and warns that the behavior may get worse when you first start.[^S11]
- *Rung 4:* The moment they stop asking, give them your full attention again. **"Want to
  help me with the laundry?"**

**Instead of:** "Fine! Just stop asking!"
**Try:** **"I said no, and I'm not going to change my mind. Do you want to help me with the
dishes or play next to me?"**

**Age tweaks:**

- **2–3:** Name it and move them along: **"No cookie. Here's your cup."** Then change the scene.
- **4–5:** Give a "yes" that's possible: **"No cookie now. Yes to an apple."**
- **6–7:** Teach them how to ask well: one time, in a calm voice. **"If you want me to think
  about it, ask me once, and I'll give you a real answer."**

**Next time:** If you really aren't sure yet, say so. **"Let me think. I'll tell you after
lunch."** A thoughtful "maybe" beats a "no" that turns into "yes" after the tenth ask.
