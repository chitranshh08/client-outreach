## Description (paste into KDP; 4,000-char limit; KDP-allowed HTML)
The first two lines are what shows before "Read more", so they carry the hook on their own:
the parent's exact situation, then "you're not a bad parent."

```html
<b>You've asked five times. Now you're yelling. And <i>now</i> they move.</b>

<p>If that sounds like your house, you're not a bad parent. You're stuck in a loop almost every family falls into: ask, repeat, repeat, yell. Then the guilt.</p>

<p>Here's something parents rarely hear: yelling isn't what makes kids listen. It just shows up at the same moment you finally follow through. <b>Keep the follow-through, drop the yell,</b> and you get the listening without the guilt.</p>

<p><i>The Listening Playbook</i> gives you the exact words, and the calm next step, for the everyday moments with kids aged 2 to 7 when nobody is upset, but nobody is listening either.</p>

<p><b>Inside, you'll find:</b></p>
<ul>
<li><b>The One Clear Ask:</b> five small steps that make your first ask the one that counts</li>
<li><b>The Follow-Through Ladder:</b> exactly what to do when they still don't, so you're never stuck after "no"</li>
<li><b>30 real moments, 30 scripts:</b> "In a minute!", "You can't make me!", toy cleanup, the 20-minute dawdle, mealtimes, whining, interrupting, back-talk, lying and more</li>
<li><b>Age tweaks for every script</b> for ages 2–3, 4–5 and 6–7</li>
<li><b>Calm consequences that teach:</b> related, small and soon, plus a Consequence Picker so you never fall back on empty threats</li>
<li><b>Rewards vs. bribes:</b> the simple difference that cuts down the bargaining</li>
<li><b>Printables:</b> pocket cards, a "When… then…" chart, a family rules poster, a 14-day tracker and a 30-script cheat sheet</li>
<li><b>The 14-Day Listening Challenge:</b> one small step a day to put it all together</li>
</ul>

<p>This is a playbook, not a textbook. Read Part 1 in one sitting, then find your moment and use it. Every script is in bold and every moment uses the same layout, so you can find the words in seconds. The guidance draws on published advice from children's health organizations, with every source listed.</p>

<p><b>Perfect for</b> parents of toddlers, preschoolers and young school-age kids who want to stop repeating themselves, stop yelling, and have fewer power struggles.</p>

<p>Book 2 in the Calm Words Parenting series. For meltdowns and big feelings, see <i>The Meltdown Playbook</i>. Each book stands on its own.</p>

<p><b>Scroll up and get your copy in time for tomorrow morning's "shoes on."</b></p>
```
