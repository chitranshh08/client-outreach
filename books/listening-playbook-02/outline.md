**Series rules for this book**
- Same title style, readability spec and card layout as Book 1 (`books/meltdown-playbook-01/outline.md`).
  A reader who knows Book 1 should feel at home on page one.
- **No repeated content.** None of Book 1's 30 moments appear here. Book 1 tools (60-Second
  Reset, 3-Line Meltdown Script, HALT) get a 2–3 sentence reminder plus a pointer to Book 1,
  never a re-taught chapter. Repeated material earns "same book twice" reviews.
- **The split, stated in the intro and Chapter 10:** upset child → Book 1. Calm child who
  won't do it → this book.

**Script card (one per moment, always this order):**
What's going on · **Say this** · **If they still don't** (follow-through ladder) ·
Instead of / Try · Age tweaks (2–3 · 4–5 · 6–7) · Next time

## PART 1: ASK ONCE, FOLLOW THROUGH

### Chapter 1: One Clear Ask: Five Steps That End the Repeat Loop (~2,000) ← quick win
- Vignette: "Shoes on" asked from the kitchen, four times
- **The One Clear Ask:** Get close · Get their eyes · Say it short (what *to* do,
  ≤ 10 words) · Wait (count five in your head) · Follow through (help them do it)
- Tell, don't ask: "Can you put your shoes on?" invites "no". "Shoes on, please." doesn't
- 5 swaps: Instead of / Try
- **Your 5-Minute Action:** use the method on the next 3 requests today
- Extra: pocket One Clear Ask card

### Chapter 2: Why Kids Tune Out (and Why Yelling Seems to Work) (~2,000)
- Switching attention away from play is hard for young brains (executive function; S1, S6)
- Too many words, too many requests, asked from across the room
- The real reason yelling "works": it's usually the moment we finally get up and follow
  through. Keep the follow-through, drop the yell
- Connection before direction (2-sentence callback to Book 1's Calm Words Formula)
- **Action:** count how many instructions you give in one hour (no judgment, just data)

### Chapter 3: What to Do When They Still Don't: The Follow-Through Ladder (~2,500)
- **The Follow-Through Ladder:** 1 One reminder, with a choice (the only repeat, and the
  warning if a consequence is coming) · 2 Helping hands or "When… then…" · 3 A calm
  consequence (related, small, soon) · 4 Move on: no lecture, no grudge
- Consequences vs punishment vs threats you won't keep (AAP S4, AACAP S8)
- Rewards vs bribes: decided before vs offered mid-standoff
- Catch the opposite: specific praise for the behavior you want (S7, S9)
- **Action:** fill in your Consequence Picker for your family's 3 hardest moments
- Extra: **Consequence Picker** (printable)

## PART 2: THE PLAYBOOK: 30 MOMENTS, 30 SCRIPTS
*Find your moment and use it.*

### Chapter 4: "In a Minute!": When They Tune You Out (~2,400)
1. Ignoring you mid-play ("Did you hear me?")
2. "Come here" / time to come inside
3. "In a minute!" (the stall)
4. Asking them to stop (jumping on the couch, banging, poking)
5. Listens to one parent (or the sitter) but not the other
6. The broken record: "Can I? Can I? Can I?"
- **Action:** pick your top 2 tune-out moments; use the card for 5 days

### Chapter 5: "You're Not the Boss of Me!": No, Defiance and Power Struggles (~2,600)
7. The flat "No!"
8. "You can't make me!"
9. Running off when you call (with a safety version for streets and parking lots)
10. Doing it while looking right at you
11. "But why?" (the little lawyer)
12. Breaking a rule you just set
- **Action:** choose one battle to drop and one to hold firmly this week

### Chapter 6: Toys Away, Hands Helping: Cleanup and Chores (~2,200)
13. Toy cleanup
14. "I can't do it!" (when they can)
15. The 20-minute dawdle
16. Bath time: getting in, and getting out
17. Helping around the house (setting the table, laundry, feeding the pet)
- **Action:** give your child one real job and a name for it ("table captain")
- Extra: "When… then…" chart

### Chapter 7: Table Talk: Mealtimes Without the Battle (~2,000)
18. Won't come to the table
19. Won't stay in the seat
20. "Yuck! I'm not eating that!" (behavior only; feeding roles from S6; no nutrition advice)
21. Playing with or dropping food
22. Forgetting please and thank you
- **Action:** one family meal with a 2-rule table plan

### Chapter 8: Whining, Interrupting and Rude Words (~2,200)
23. The whiny voice
24. Interrupting when you're on the phone or talking
25. Potty words and silly-rude talk
26. "You're stupid!" / "Go away!" (rude, not upset; if upset, see Book 1 Moment 30)
27. Eye-rolls and back-talk (ages 5–7)
- **Action:** teach the "hand on my arm" interrupt signal

### Chapter 9: Trickier Moments (~1,800)
28. Not listening at a friend's house or a playdate
29. Touching everything in a store
30. "I didn't do it!" (lying)
- **Action:** plan your exit line for the next visit

### Chapter 10: When They Still Won't Listen (~1,800)
- Judge progress by fewer repeats, not zero repeats
- Fewer instructions per day; are the adults saying the same thing?
- Check underneath: HALT and big changes (Book 1 reminder, 2 lines)
- When "won't" turns into a meltdown → switch to Book 1's 3-Line Script
- When to talk to your pediatrician: if your child often seems not to hear you, or
  listening is hard everywhere (home, daycare, school). General info, clear disclaimer,
  no diagnosis (S1, S2)
- **Action:** note which step of the ladder made the biggest difference

## PART 3: A HOME THAT LISTENS

### Chapter 11: Fewer Reminders: Let the Routine Do the Talking (~1,800)
- 3–5 family rules, worded as what to do ("We use gentle hands")
- Picture routines and "What's next?" (one line pointing to Book 1's routine chart)
- Warnings that work: concrete and short ("two more pages, then bath")
- Jobs, not chores: belonging builds cooperation
- **Action:** write your family rules together and post them
- Extra: family rules poster

### Chapter 12: Your 14-Day Listening Challenge (~1,300)
- One small step a day for two weeks, building to the full method + ladder
- Day 14: check your repeat count vs Day 1
- Extra: 14-day tracker
- **Review ask goes right after this chapter** (peak satisfaction; quality bar)
