## Reader avatar
Same parent as Book 1, so the books sell to each other.
- **Who:** A parent (usually mom, 25–40) of one or two kids aged 2–7.
- **Situation now:** She asks for the same thing five times: shoes on, toys away, come to
  the table, stop jumping on the couch. By the fifth time she's yelling. Then the child
  listens, and she hates that yelling is the thing that works.
- **Already tried:** Counting to three, threats she doesn't follow through on, sticker
  charts that fizzled after a week, bribes, timeouts, "I'm not going to say it again" (and
  saying it again). Gentle parenting accounts tell her to "connect", but not what to do
  when the child still says no.
- **What she wants (her words):** "Why do I have to yell before they listen?" "I'm so
  tired of repeating myself." "I want them to just do it without a fight." "I don't want
  to be the mean mom, but I can't be a pushover either."

## The promise (one sentence)
> You'll know exactly what to say, and what to do next, to get your child to listen and
> cooperate without yelling, nagging, threats or bribes.

Deliberately not promised: "listen the first time", "every time", "in 3 days". Those win
clicks and lose reviews.

## Competitor gaps → how we win
_Hypotheses from the market shape and public descriptions. Replace with 1–3★ review quotes
once the owner sends review screenshots (strategy report, section 4)._

| Gap (hypothesis) | Our fix |
|---|---|
| The classics teach principles and skills; parents still have to work out the words for their own moment | Lookup by moment: 30 everyday moments, each with the exact words |
| Advice stops at the request. Parents fail at the next step: "and if they still don't?" | Every script card has an **If they still don't** step: a calm follow-through ladder, so no moment ends in a standoff or a yell |
| "Use consequences" without saying which ones, so parents fall back on threats and taking everything away | A **Consequence Picker**: the related, small, immediate consequence for common moments, plus what to say while you follow through |
| Long books written as courses or article collections (one is 31 collected articles, with some repetition) | A playbook: Part 1 in ~20 minutes, then find your moment |
| Indie "stop shouting" books are generic tip lists | Same proven card format as Book 1, age tweaks for 2–3 / 4–5 / 6–7, worked examples |
| Assumes the parent stays calm | Reuses Book 1's 60-Second Reset in one paragraph (no repeated chapters) and links to Book 1 for meltdowns |

## Positioning
*How to Talk So Little Kids Will Listen* and *No Bad Kids* are the books parents trust.
**The Listening Playbook is the one they keep on the counter**: open to the moment, say the
words, follow the ladder. It sits next to the classics and next to Book 1 in "Customers
also bought", not against them.

**Series split, said plainly in both books:**
- *The Meltdown Playbook*: when your child is upset (feelings are running the show)
- *The Listening Playbook*: when your child is fine but won't do what you asked

## Format & business decisions
- **Target word count:** ~25,000–28,000 (Book 1 landed at ~26k; ≈ 110–125 pages at 6×9).
  No padding: chapter lengths follow the content.
- **Trim size:** 6×9 in, white paper, B&W interior. Same build pipeline as Book 1
  (`tools/book_build/`), so the books match on the shelf.
- **KDP Select:** **Yes.** 8/10 page-1 books in the parent niche are in KU, and KU readers
  who borrowed Book 1 can borrow Book 2 at no extra cost to them (page reads for us).
- **Price:** ebook $2.99 launch (2 weeks) → $5.99 · paperback $14.99. Same as Book 1, so the
  series looks consistent.
- **Quick win (first 10%):** the **Say-It-Once Method** (5 steps) in Chapter 1, usable at
  the next "put your shoes on".
- **Extras:** 30 script cards, the Consequence Picker, a pocket Say-It-Once card, family
  rules poster (3–5 rules, worded as what *to* do), a "When… then…" chart, a 14-day
  Say-It-Once Challenge tracker, the 30 Scripts Cheat Sheet.
- **Cover:** same family as Book 1 (cream, navy title, coral speech bubble). New
  illustration: an ear with a speech bubble, or a child's hand in a parent's hand.
  `cover-brief.md` comes at the packaging stage.
