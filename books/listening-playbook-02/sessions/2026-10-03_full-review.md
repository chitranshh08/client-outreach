## Warnings (fix before publishing)

**W1. Food is used as leverage in a few "When… then…" lines, which breaks the book's own rules.**
- Locations: Ch 3, Rung 2 (`04-follow-through-ladder.md`): *"When the bricks are in the bin,
  then we'll have dinner."* · Moment 2, Rung 2: *"When you're inside, then we'll have the
  snack."* · Moment 27, Rung 2: *"…then you can have your snack."* · Moment 15, Next time:
  *"get dressed before anything fun, like breakfast or a show."*
- Problem: Ch 3 says a consequence "never takes away something your child needs: food…",
  and Ch 7 says "Dinner is at dinner time." Making dinner wait for cleanup contradicts both.
- Why it matters: a careful reader (or reviewer) spots the contradiction; feeding battles
  are a sensitive topic and a 1★ risk.
- Fix: swap the "then" for a non-food next step. *"When the bricks are in the bin, then we'll
  go outside."* · *"When you're inside, then you can pick the game."* · *"When your backpack is
  on the hook, then you can go play."* · Moment 15: *"like a show or playtime."*

**W2. Part 2 readers meet "Rung 1 / Rung 2" without knowing what the rungs are.**
- Location: Part 2 opener, "How to use a script card" (`05-in-a-minute.md`).
- Problem: the book tells parents to jump straight to their moment, but every card uses
  rung numbers defined only in Ch 3.
- Why it matters: a tired parent at the fifth "shoes on" won't flip back to Ch 3.
- Fix: add a four-line ladder reminder under the card description (≈ 35 words):
  *Rung 1: one reminder, with a choice · Rung 2: helping hands or "When… then…" · Rung 3: a
  calm consequence · Rung 4: move on.*

**W3. Action notes switch between (parentheses) and [brackets].**
- Locations: Ch 1 examples and swaps (*"(Walk over. Crouch.)"*), Ch 3 Rung 2 (*"(Then gently
  guide their hands…)"*) vs. Part 2, which says *"Everything in brackets is what to do."*
- Why it matters: parents learn one visual code; mixing them makes scripts slower to scan.
- Fix: use [brackets] for every action note in Part 1, matching Part 2.

**W4. The intro's first reassurance can be read the wrong way.**
- Location: Start Here, line 17: *"you're not doing this wrong because you're a bad parent."*
- Problem: it can read as "you *are* doing this wrong (just not because you're bad)".
- Why it matters: it's the most important emotional line in the book (reader-psychology §2).
- Fix: *"**You're not a bad parent.** You're stuck in a loop that almost every family falls
  into."* (Word-neutral, so the quick win stays at 9%.)

**W5. "The Sunday box until tomorrow" contradicts itself.**
- Location: Moment 13, Rung 3: *"…going in the Sunday box until tomorrow."*
- Fix: *"the put-away box until tomorrow."* (Also update the cheat sheet if it's mentioned.)

**W6. Consequence Picker labels clash with the numbered moments.**
- Location: Ch 3 printable: *"Moment 1: ____ / Moment 2 / Moment 3"*.
- Problem: Part 2 has Moments 1–30, so "Moment 1" here looks like a reference to Moment 1
  ("Did you hear me?").
- Fix: *"Hard moment A / B / C."*

## Suggestions

**S1. Some lines promise or generalize more than the sources support.** (Quality bar §3:
facts sourced; brief: confident, not magical.)
- Ch 3: *"once you know it, you'll never again have to wonder what comes next"* → "you'll
  always have a next calm step." · *"Most of the time, you'll stop at the first or second
  rung"* and *"That's how it goes most of the time"* → "often".
- Ch 2: *"That's where most families slide back"*, *"Most parents are surprised by both
  numbers"*, *"Children who feel seen are more willing to switch gears"* → "many"/"often"/cut.
- Ch 1: *"Very often… your child starts to move"*, *"it usually ends with both of you upset"*
  → "often"/"can end".
- Moment 7 *"Many toddlers say no and then cooperate"* (fine), Moment 12 *"Toddlers can't
  resist a ball"* → "find it very hard to resist", Moment 14 *"usually a request for
  connection"* → "often", Moment 15 *"keeps them on track better than 'hurry'"* → "helps
  keep them on track", Ch 7 *"young children don't feel hungry on a schedule"* → "don't always".

**S2. Three sentences are long enough to slow a tired reader.** Split each in two:
- Ch 2, AAP sentence (≈ 35 words). · Ch 2, ZERO TO THREE sentence (≈ 33). · Moment 30, Kang
  Lee sentence (≈ 40).

**S3. Reading-time promise is a little low.** Part 1 is ≈ 5,250 words (≈ 25 minutes at a
normal pace) but says "about 20 minutes" in the intro and the Part 1 opener. Fix: "about 25
minutes".

**S4. Ch 1 example label doesn't match its script.** *"Turning off the bath tap"* introduces a
getting-out-of-the-bath script. Fix: "Getting out of the bath".

**S5. Moment 13's "Try" line breaks the one-thing-at-a-time rule.** *"Blocks in the bin. Then
books on the shelf. Then we're done."* Fix: *"Blocks in the bin, please."* (next step after).

**S6. Moment 23 models a small fib.** *"I can't understand the whiny voice."* Fix: *"It's hard
for me to listen to the whiny voice."*

**S7. Moment 11 gives a made-up reason.** *"…because the sugar bugs come out at night."* The
book teaches honesty in Moment 30. Fix: *"Teeth now, so they stay clean and strong."*

**S8. Ch 3's "never take away… their time with you" needs one clarifying clause.** Moments 6,
23, 24, 26 ask parents to briefly turn their attention away. Fix: add *"(A short pause in
attention for whining or pestering is different. See Chapter 8.)"*

**S9. No help for the yell that still happens.** Guilt after yelling is a top pain
(reader-psychology §2), and the intro admits "you'll still raise your voice sometimes," but
Book 2 never says what to do next. Readers without Book 1 have no repair step.
- Fix: add a short "If you yelled anyway" box to Ch 10 (≈ 100 words, no new facts): get
  calm, say *"I yelled. I'm sorry. I was frustrated, and it's my job to stay calm. Let's try
  again,"* then redo the ask the One Clear Ask way. Point to Book 1's Repair Script.

**S10. "Large national study" overstates the sample.** Ch 2: Straus & Field surveyed ≈ 1,000
parents (national, representative). Fix: "a national study".

## Notes (optional)

- **N1.** Front matter says *"Ignoring you in the middle of play."* The book reframes
  "ignoring," but it's the word parents use when they look for their moment. Keep.
- **N2.** Moment 5 tweak *"With me, the rule is…"* slightly undercuts "same words for all
  adults," but it's realistic for split households. Keep.
- **N3.** Ch 7: *"put the food on the table, at the table, at mealtime"* repeats "table."
  Tidy to "serve the food at the table at mealtime."
- **N4.** Two children at once (Ch 11 opener) isn't covered as a moment. Good candidate for
  a sibling-focused Book 3.
- **N5.** Any fix that adds words before Ch 1's step list (W2 doesn't; W4, S3 are
  word-neutral) moves the quick win toward the 10% line. Keep front-matter additions at zero.
