# Outline: The Meltdown Playbook

Target: ~30,000 words (≈ 140 pages, 6×9) · 3 parts · 13 chapters · 30 script cards
Quick win: Chapter 1 starts at ~word 1,300, inside the first 10% (≈ 3,000 words), so it's
in the Look Inside sample.

## Title style rules (why the headings read the way they do)
- **Speak in the parent's or child's own words.** Headings are things parents actually hear
  or think ("I don't want to!", "Everyone's staring"). Recognition beats description.
- **Confident, not magical.** Promise a clear method and real words, never instant or
  guaranteed results ("in 60 seconds, every time"). Overpromising is the #1 cause of
  "didn't work for my kid" 1★ reviews. Confidence comes from specifics: "4 sentences",
  "30 moments", "the script".
- **Scannable.** A parent mid-meltdown should find their situation from the contents page
  in under 10 seconds.

## Readability spec (for the reader: tired parents, reading in short bursts)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Grade 6–8 reading level; average sentence < 18 words; paragraphs ≤ 3 lines | Read at 10 pm, in a waiting room, one-handed on a phone |
| Every moment fits on 1–2 pages and reads in under 3 minutes | Findable and usable mid-crisis |
| **The words to say are always bold** (or in a box in print) | The eye finds the script instantly |
| Same card layout for all 30 moments | Once learned, every page is familiar |
| Short vignette (3–4 sentences) opens each chapter: "It's 5:40 pm. Maya, 4, is face-down on the kitchen floor…" | Recognition makes the reader feel seen, and it hooks |
| No more than one "why" paragraph per moment | Parents want the what; the why lives in Chapter 2 |
| **Ebook: no tables.** "Instead of / Try" becomes two short lines; tables only in the paperback | Tables render badly on phones and e-ink Kindles, a common formatting complaint in reviews |
| Printables reproduced in the book *and* as an optional download link | Paperback readers can photocopy; ebook readers need the link |
| Warm, zero-guilt tone; "you" not "parents should" | Guilt-heavy parenting books get "made me feel worse" reviews |
| Reads well aloud | Ready for an audiobook later |
