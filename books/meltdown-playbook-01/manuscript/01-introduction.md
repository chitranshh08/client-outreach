# Start Here: You're Not a Bad Parent. You Just Need the Words.

It's 5:40 on a Tuesday. Dinner is half made. Your four-year-old wanted to press the
elevator button, and his sister pressed it first. Now he is face-down on the kitchen
floor, screaming like the world has ended.

You know you're supposed to stay calm. You've read the posts. You've saved the videos.
But right now, in this moment, your mind is blank. So you say the thing you swore you'd
never say. Or you raise your voice. Or you hand him your phone just to make it stop.

Later, after bedtime, you replay it and feel awful.

If that sounds like your house, I want you to hear this first: **you are not a bad
parent.** You are a tired parent who was never given the words. That's a very different
problem, and it's one we can fix.

## Why you freeze in the moment

Here's something most parenting advice skips. When your child melts down, your own
stress rises too. Your heart speeds up. Your patience drains. And the part of your brain
that is good at finding gentle, clever words is exactly the part that gets harder to
reach when you're stressed.

That's why "just stay calm and connect" doesn't help much at 5:40 pm. You can't invent
calm words while you're flooded. You can only *remember* them.

So this book gives you words to remember. Short, simple scripts you can learn when life
is quiet and pull out when it isn't.

## What this book is

This is a playbook, not a textbook. It's built to be used, not just read.

- **Part 1 (Chapters 1 to 3)** gives you the foundation: how to calm yourself first,
  what's really going on inside your child during a meltdown, and a simple four-step
  formula that works for almost any hard moment. Read this part first. It takes about
  20 minutes, and you can start using it tonight.
- **Part 2 (Chapters 4 to 10)** is the playbook itself: 30 real moments, from the
  checkout-lane candy standoff to "I hate you!", each with the exact words to say.
  Don't read it cover to cover. Find your moment and use it.
- **Part 3 (Chapters 11 to 13)** helps you have fewer meltdowns in the first place. You'll
  teach feelings while everyone is calm, learn how to repair things after you lose your
  temper, and follow a simple 30-day plan.

Every moment in Part 2 follows the same layout, so once you've used one, you know how to
use them all. The words to say are always in **bold**. If you only have ten seconds,
read the bold line and go.

## What this book is not

This book is for everyday meltdowns in children aged about 2 to 7. It is not therapy, and
it can't diagnose anything. Some children need more help than any book can give, and
that is nobody's fault. Chapter 10 explains the signs that it's time to talk to your
pediatrician.

It also isn't a promise that your child will never melt down again. Meltdowns are a
normal part of growing up. The goal is shorter storms, calmer adults, and a child who
slowly learns to handle big feelings, with you right beside them.

## A few notes before you start

**The scripts are starting points.** Change the words so they sound like you. A script
you actually say beats a perfect one you forget.

**Some scripts won't work the first time.** That's normal. Children test new responses.
Use the same words for a week before you decide whether they help.

**The families in this book are illustrative.** Maya, Leo and the other children you'll
meet are composites, built from the kinds of moments almost every family knows. They are
not real people.

**You don't have to be perfect.** You will still lose your temper sometimes. Every parent
does. Chapter 12 shows you how to repair those moments, and repair turns out
to be one of the best things you can teach your child.

## Your first step

Before you turn the page, think about the last week at home. Which moment happened most?
Leaving somewhere fun? Screens off? Bedtime? Hitting a sibling?

Find it in the **Find Your Moment** list at the front of this book and circle it, fold
the page corner, or add a bookmark. That's the moment we'll work on first.

Then read Chapter 1. It's short, and it will give you something to try before
tomorrow's first meltdown.
