# Zero-Budget Go-to-Market Strategy (Calm Words Parenting)

_Written 2026-10-04 for the owner. Budget assumption: near $0 in cash, 5–15 hours a week.
Facts marked (verify) change often; check them before relying on them._

## The bottom line

**Don't buy attention. Build the flywheel Amazon already runs for free, and feed it with
content we've already written.**

1. **Sell a series, not a book.** Two books for the same parent, linked, launched close
   together, each sending readers to the other. KU readers borrow both, so every reader we
   win pays twice.
2. **Win a small pond.** Child Discipline is a category where about 2–5 sales a day reach the
   top 3 (rescore 2026-10-03). Concentrate the launch into a few days there and earn the
   category badge, which keeps converting browsers for free.
3. **Turn the book into marketing.** The 60 script lines in the two books are ready-made
   "Say this, not that" posts. Pinterest and Instagram cost time, not money.
4. **Own the audience.** A free printable in the back of both books feeds an email list.
   Every future book launches to that list on day one, for $0.
5. **Spend the only real money on the cover.** Price, keywords and ads can be fixed later.
   A weak cover can't be out-marketed.
6. **Ads only when the books pay for them.** No ad spend until reviews and read-through
   prove the books convert.

## Where free attention comes from on Amazon

- **New-release visibility:** new books get extra exposure in their first weeks. Many indie
  authors report a drop-off around day 30. That's a common observation, not an Amazon
  rule, but it's the reason to launch hard and release books close together.
- **Category bestseller lists and the badge:** a top-3 spot in a small category puts the book
  in front of browsers and adds a "#1" label to the page.
- **"Customers also bought" and the series page:** the more often the same readers buy or
  borrow both books, the more Amazon shows them together.
- **Followers:** readers who follow the author on Amazon get emailed about new releases.
  Author Central is free.
- **Outside traffic:** clicks that arrive from outside Amazon and convert are tracked for free
  with **Amazon Attribution**, which is open to KDP authors. Use it on every outside link so
  we know which channel works.

## The plays, ranked by impact per hour

### Play 1: Launch the series, not two separate books (≈ 3 hours, $0)
- Upload Book 2 as soon as the cover is ready, so its launch lands **inside Book 1's first
  30 days**. Both books then share the same launch window.
- In KDP, link both books to the series "Calm Words Parenting" so the series page and the
  "Book 2 of 2" label appear.
- Once Book 2 has an ASIN: update Book 1's "What's Next" page to point to Book 2, fill in
  both books' review links in `book.json`, rebuild and re-upload. Readers can then reach the
  review page and the next book in one tap.
- **A+ Content** (free, under KDP Marketing): one "Which book do I need?" comparison module on
  both books, plus one image of a real script card. Shoppers who scroll get a reason to buy
  both.
- Set up **Author Central** for Hannah Rowe (photo, bio, both books) so the Follow button
  works.

### Play 2: Price the series like a funnel (≈ 30 minutes, $0)
- **Book 1 becomes the entry book at $2.99 permanently** (still the 70% royalty band).
  `docs/kdp-rules.md` already allows series Book 1 to go lower to feed read-through. Book 2
  stays at $5.99 after its launch window. Paperbacks stay at $14.99, which makes both ebooks
  look like a deal.
- **Timing rule that affects us:** a Kindle Countdown Deal needs the list price **unchanged
  for 30 days before and 14 days after** the deal. Every price change resets that clock. So:
  set the long-term prices early, and plan Countdown Deals at least 30 days after the last
  price change.

### Play 3: The honest-review engine (≈ 2 hours a week, $0–20)
Reviews are the bottleneck: almost no one buys a parenting book with zero reviews.
- Amazon allows **advance review copies of books**: a free copy, given with no requirement to
  review and no requirement that the review be positive. That is our only review channel.
- **Target:** 20–30 real parents on an ARC list before Book 2 goes live, aiming for 10+
  honest reviews in the first 60 days. Expect some never to post; that's normal.
- **Where to find them (free):** the pen name's Instagram/Pinterest followers, and parenting
  communities **that allow** ARC calls (read each group's rules first; many ban promotion).
  Offer the book, not a review: *"I'm looking for parents of 2–7-year-olds to read an
  early copy. If it helps you, an honest review is welcome, but there's no obligation."*
- **Delivery:** email the EPUB/PDF directly, or use BookFunnel's low-cost plan (≈ $20/year,
  verify) for cleaner downloads.
- **Never** (CLAUDE.md rule 2): paid reviews, review swaps, family or friends, gift cards,
  asking only happy readers, or asking for 5 stars.

### Play 4: The content flywheel (≈ 3 hours a week, $0)
We already have 60 tested scripts (30 per book). Each one becomes a post:

| Format | Example | Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script card (vertical image) | **Instead of:** "How many times do I have to tell you?" **Try:** [Walk over.] "Coat on, please. I'll wait." | **Pinterest** (first priority) | Parents search Pinterest for exactly this; pins keep getting found for months |
| 5-slide carousel | "The One Clear Ask in 5 steps" | Instagram | Saves and shares spread it; one carousel per tool |
| 20-second talking-text video | "Why yelling *seems* to work" | Instagram Reels / TikTok / Shorts | The book's core insight is a natural hook |

- **Rule of thumb:** give the script away, sell the system. Each post solves one moment and
  says the book has 29 more. That's honest and it's how practical books spread.
- **Cadence:** batch 2 hours a week in Canva's free plan (one template, brand colors from
  `pen-names/hannah-rowe.md`) to make 7 pins + 2 carousels; schedule them; 15 minutes a day
  replying to comments.
- Every bio and pin links to the book through an **Amazon Attribution** link, one per
  channel, so the data tells us where to keep spending time.

### Play 5: Own the audience with a free printable (≈ 4 hours once, $0)
- **Reader magnet:** "The Calm Words Pocket Pack": both books' pocket cards, the Consequence
  Picker and the family rules poster as one printable PDF. (These pages already exist.)
- **Where it's offered:** the back matter of both books ("Get the printable pack"), the
  Instagram and Pinterest bios, and every few posts.
- **Tool:** **Kit's free plan** (up to 10,000 subscribers with free landing pages, verify).
  MailerLite's free plan dropped to 250 subscribers in 2026, so it's the wrong choice now.
- **What the list gets:** one script a week by email. When Book 3 launches, the list hears
  first. That's a launch-day spike that costs nothing.

### Play 6: The KDP Select promo calendar (≈ 1 hour per promo, $0)
- **Free days (up to 5 per 90 days, ebook only):** run 2–3 free days on **Book 1** about a
  week after Book 2 goes live. Free readers who like Book 1 find Book 2 already waiting, at
  full price or in KU.
- **Free promo sites:** several deal newsletters still accept free submissions for free days
  (lists such as Indies Today's track who's free vs paid). Submit 1–2 weeks ahead and check
  each site's current terms; many have minimum-review rules, which is another reason Play 3
  comes first.
- **Countdown Deals:** schedule only after the 30-day price-stability window (Play 2).

### Play 7: Release the next book fast (ongoing)
- The cheapest ad for a series is the next book in it. Every new book gets its own launch
  window and sends readers back to the earlier ones.
- **Book 3 target:** live within 6–8 weeks of Book 2, chosen from data (sibling fighting or
  the worried child; see the Book 2 strategy report).
- After Book 3: an ebook bundle of Books 1–3 at a higher price (T.R. Fosters' 6-in-1 bundle
  shows the format exists in this niche).

### Play 8: Ads, only when the books pay for them
- **Start conditions (all three):** Book 2 has 10+ reviews at 4.3★ or above, read-through
  from Book 1 to Book 2 is measured, and royalties from the past month cover the test.
- **First test:** one automatic campaign plus one product-targeting campaign aimed at
  competitor pages (*No Bad Kids*, *How to Talk So Little Kids Will Listen*, *Talk, Don't
  Yell*), $3–5 a day for 14 days, paid from royalties.
- **Kill rule:** pause any target that spends more than one book's royalty without an order.

## What we won't do
- Anything that breaks Amazon's review rules (Play 3 list) or uses another author's name in
  ads or metadata as a keyword.
- Buying followers, "promotion" gigs, or mass self-promotion in groups that don't allow it.
  It burns the pen name.
- Giveaways of devices or gift cards tied to reviews.
- Spending on ads before reviews exist. Paid clicks to a zero-review page mostly waste money.

## Honest expectations
Most KDP books sell very little (CLAUDE.md). This plan doesn't guarantee sales. It makes the
most of the free levers, builds assets that compound (reviews, followers, an email list,
more books), and only spends money once the data says it will come back. The first 60 days
are about reviews and read-through, not income.
